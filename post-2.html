🎓 Introduction

Learning to trade can become confusing very quickly. There are hundreds of indicators, strategies, concepts, trading styles, and market theories available online.

The purpose of this curriculum is to provide a structured learning path that moves from the fundamentals of financial markets toward advanced market analysis and professional execution.

Instead of attempting to learn everything at once, the curriculum is divided into ten stages. Each stage builds on concepts introduced earlier.

The goal is not to find a single strategy that supposedly predicts every market movement. The goal is to develop a strong understanding of price, structure, risk, liquidity, market behavior, and disciplined execution.

🏆 Priority Order: What Should You Learn First?

You do not need to master all 36 modules immediately. A better approach is to build your knowledge from the fundamentals upward.

  1. Price Action — Understand how price behaves before adding complex tools.
  2. Market Structure — Learn HH, HL, LH, LL and structural changes.
  3. Risk Management — Learn how to control risk before focusing on setups.
  4. Multi-Timeframe Analysis — Learn how different timeframes provide context.
  5. Liquidity — Study where significant areas of market interest may exist.
  6. FVG + Order Blocks — Explore these concepts after understanding structure and liquidity.
  7. Volume Analysis — Add participation and activity-based information.
  8. ICT Time Concepts — Study session and timing concepts as an advanced framework.
  9. Wyckoff — Explore market-cycle and accumulation/distribution concepts.
  10. Volume Profile / AMT — Develop a deeper understanding of value and auction behavior.
  11. Order Flow — Study execution-level market activity.
  12. Sentiment & On-Chain — Add broader market information, particularly for crypto.
  13. Elliott Wave — Treat as an optional specialization rather than a foundation.

📚 How to Use This Curriculum

A curriculum is useful only when the concepts are actually studied and tested. Avoid trying to memorize every terminology at once.

  1. Learn the concept.
  2. Study examples on historical charts.
  3. Write down your observations.
  4. Test the idea using historical data or simulation.
  5. Record the results.
  6. Review mistakes and refine your understanding.

This process is much more useful than continuously switching between different strategies without collecting evidence about how they perform.

⚠️ Important Risk Note

This curriculum is provided for educational purposes only. Financial markets involve substantial risk and no strategy, indicator, framework, or trading concept can guarantee profits.

Concepts such as leverage, futures, margin, and liquidation can significantly increase risk. Beginners should focus on education, historical analysis, and simulated practice rather than attempting to generate immediate profits.

Always treat risk management and capital preservation as core parts of trading education.

🎯 Conclusion

Becoming knowledgeable about trading is a long-term learning process. There is no single indicator, strategy, or framework that replaces a solid understanding of markets.

Start with the fundamentals: price action, market structure, risk management, and multi-timeframe analysis. Once those foundations are strong, gradually explore liquidity, technical tools, market-cycle theories, order flow, and other advanced frameworks.

The objective should not simply be to find more trading concepts. The objective is to develop a structured, testable, and disciplined process for understanding markets and managing uncertainty.

Learn → Test → Record → Review → Improve.
