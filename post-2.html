Introduction

Trading is a broad subject that combines market mechanics, price action, risk management, technical analysis, psychology, and disciplined execution.

Instead of learning random indicators or isolated strategies, a structured curriculum makes it easier to understand how different concepts connect with one another.

This curriculum is organized into ten stages, beginning with basic market concepts and gradually moving toward advanced topics such as market structure, liquidity, volume profile, order flow, and professional execution.

Educational note: This curriculum is intended for educational purposes. Trading financial markets involves substantial risk. Concepts should be studied and practiced in a simulated environment before considering any real-money activity.

🏆 Recommended Learning Order

Trying to learn every trading concept simultaneously can become overwhelming. A more structured approach is to build knowledge progressively.

  1. Price Action
  2. Market Structure
  3. Risk Management
  4. Multi-Timeframe Analysis
  5. Liquidity Concepts
  6. FVG & Order Blocks
  7. Volume Analysis
  8. Time-Based Market Concepts
  9. Wyckoff
  10. Volume Profile / AMT
  11. Order Flow
  12. Sentiment & On-Chain Analysis
  13. Elliott Wave — optional specialization

Conclusion

A strong understanding of markets comes from building knowledge step by step rather than searching for a single strategy or indicator.

Start with market mechanics, price action, structure and risk management. Once those foundations are clear, gradually explore more specialized frameworks such as liquidity analysis, Wyckoff, volume profile and order flow.

Most importantly, focus on developing a repeatable learning and review process. Use historical charts, simulated environments, journaling and statistical review to test whether your ideas actually hold up over time.

Remember: No trading framework guarantees profits. Risk management, critical thinking and evidence-based testing are more important than memorizing terminology.
